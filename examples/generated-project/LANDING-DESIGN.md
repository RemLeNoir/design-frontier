# LANDING-DESIGN.md

> Public product communication contract for Frontier Ops.

## Objective

Explain the product to the intended audience, show credible evidence and move qualified visitors toward one clear next action.

## Narrative order

1. What the product does and for whom.
2. Visible evidence of the product or workflow.
3. Capabilities grouped by user problem.
4. Proof, trust and objections.
5. Conversion action with a clear expectation.

## Visual rhythm

- Public pages may use more whitespace and larger type than the application.
- Large sections require real content, product evidence or a narrative transition.
- Brand expression cannot reduce comprehension, accessibility or performance.
- Motion is optional and never required to understand the message.

## Product evidence

- Prefer real interface crops, diagrams, examples or concrete outputs.
- Decorative mockups cannot be the only proof.
- Screenshots reflect the current product and the application design contract.
- Product images use consistent framing, crop rules and captions.

## Calls to action

- Use one primary CTA label consistently.
- Explain what happens after activation when the commitment is not obvious.
- Secondary CTAs do not compete equally with the primary path.
- Conversion components define success, error and loading states.

## Boundaries

- Accessibility requirements override all aesthetic references.
- A reference may influence only the layer where it is selected.
- Marketing spacing, typography and motion do not redefine application density.
- Existing component behavior overrides purely visual inspiration unless a migration is explicit.
- Third-party references never authorize copying proprietary assets, source code or exact compositions.
- Use as structural reference, not as permission to ship every available widget.
- Do not inherit demo-page visual clutter or oversized cards.
- Remove unused navigation levels and decorative dashboard blocks.
- Do not transfer marketing headline scale into the application.
- Do not assume its issue-tracking interaction model fits every domain.
- Low visual noise must not reduce discoverability for novice users.
- Use view-switching patterns only when multiple representations add real value.
- Do not expose database concepts to users who do not need them.
- Table flexibility must not replace explicit workflow design.
- Use behavior and component rigor without importing IBM brand identity.
- Enterprise density still requires task prioritization.
- Do not combine multiple complete component systems in one project.
- Use trust and clarity patterns without copying payment-specific semantics.
- Decorative gradients belong to marketing surfaces, not operational forms by default.
- Documentation density must remain appropriate for the user's expertise.
- Adapt visual identity while preserving interaction clarity.
- Do not copy government branding into unrelated products.
- Service simplicity should not erase necessary expert controls.
- Use operational patterns without inheriting Shopify-specific vocabulary.
- Contextual actions must remain predictable outside commerce workflows.
- Resource-list cards should not replace tables when comparison is primary.
- Never use as a table, form-density or backoffice-shell reference.
- Large type and whitespace require sufficient product imagery and concise copy.
- Do not reproduce proprietary assets, fonts or product-page compositions wholesale.
- Display type, public spacing and animation do not redefine application screens.
- References do not authorize copying proprietary assets or exact sections.
